Plastic doesn't disappear. It just outlives us.

Most plastic ever made still exists somewhere on the planet. Conventional recycling can't keep pace with it. Geoxs Biotech engineers enzymes and microbes that break plastic down at the molecular level, turning waste back into raw material instead of pollution.

Alongside degradation, we're developing algae-derived bioplastic materials — a complementary platform that gives industries a biodegradable alternative from the outset, not just a way to clean up afterward.

9%

of plastic waste is recycled worldwide, according to OECD estimates. The rest is landfilled, incinerated, or left in the environment.

How the science works

Four connected disciplines, working from enzyme discovery up to recovered material and sustainable bioplastics — backed by active IP.

MODULE

Enzyme Discovery & Engineering

We screen microbial enzymes, including esterases and hydrolases, for activity against plastic polymers like PET, then engineer them to work faster and hold up outside a lab beaker. The work is backed by existing IP, with an extended patent underway covering our newly developed prototype-based degradation system.

Microbial Bioprocessing

Fermentation systems that grow the organisms producing these enzymes at a scale beyond a research bench.

Waste-to-Resource Recovery

Breakdown byproducts are routed back into raw material streams industries can actually use.

Algae-Derived Bioplastics

A complementary material platform: algae-based feedstock engineered into biodegradable bioplastics that address pollution before it starts.

1

Collect

Plastic waste streams are sourced and characterized.

2

Screen

Microbial candidates are tested for polymer-degrading activity.

3

Engineer

Promising enzymes are refined for speed and stability.

4

Recover

Breakdown products are captured as reusable raw material using our developed prototype system.
